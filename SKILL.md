# Result-led Content Positioning

Help the user decide **who the content serves, what result it promises, and where a tool belongs**. The audience should get value without first caring which model or product was used.

## Use this skill when

- The user is defining an account or channel around practical outcomes, work, or independent case studies.
- Titles lead with a model name, version, or feature instead of the audience's problem.
- The user wants to turn a real project, product demo, customer problem, or failed attempt into a content angle.

Do not use it to verify claims or statistics, research a topic, or produce a complete script and filming package. Route those tasks to the appropriate research or content-production workflow.

## Workflow

1. **Define the audience task.** Write: "[who] wants [observable result] in [situation] but is blocked by [obstacle]." Remove model and product names; the idea should still be useful.
2. **Choose an evidence hook.** Select a result, before-and-after, demonstration, real data point, failure, or time cost that can actually be shown. Match the title's promise to that evidence.
3. **Choose the framing.** For search-oriented content, name the durable problem and result. For recommendation-oriented content, open with a concrete change or demonstration. Do not trade accuracy for a stronger hook.
4. **Place the product in the process.** Mention a product only where it demonstrably helps. Do not make the title a product announcement unless that is genuinely the audience's task.
5. **Check the evidence boundary.** If there is no real outcome or process evidence, propose a test or clearly label the content as a demo. Do not invent a success story.

## Boundaries

- Do not treat any single content format, sales pattern, or product-placement choice as a universal formula.
- Do not fabricate income, customers, results, or credentials.
- Do not use disguised spelling, false official identity, or moderation-evasion tactics.
- Do not present observational examples as proof that a format causes views, sales, or conversion.
- Watch for titles that name only a tool, body copy that becomes an unsupported advertisement, and failures rewritten as fake success.
